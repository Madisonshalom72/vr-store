Videos produced: #1 and #6. Scripts for all six below; #2–#5 are production slots.

## #1 · Set Up Your Store — From Empty Room to Open for Business  [PRODUCED]
**Runtime:** ~2 min · **Promise:** Stand up your storefront, stock the shelves, and take your first order — human and agent.
**Visuals:** the prototype itself — storefront glide, shelf tap, back-end inventory, agent checkout.

**Narration script:**
> Welcome to your store. In the next two minutes, you'll go from an empty room to open for business.
>
> This is the storefront — a real, navigable store in the browser. No headset, no app install. Your customers glide down the aisles, tap a product on the shelf, and buy it, right on their phone.
>
> Every product here comes from one catalog. Name, price, stock — one source of truth.
>
> Now the back end. This is where you run it. Add an item once — its name, its price, its stock — and it appears on the 3D shelf, in the browse list, and in the machine-readable catalog that AI agents read. Edit a price here, and it changes everywhere, instantly.
>
> Your store is born one of two ways. Build it virtual, and it gets an address in the world — a district, a street, a number, and a link you can share anywhere. Or scan your real shop: upload a walkthrough, and your actual space becomes the store.
>
> And watch this: an AI agent just read the same catalog, placed an order, and paid in full — in USDC, through the x402 payment flow. Humans pay by card. Same shelf, same stock, one order list.
>
> That's setup. Your store is open — to people, and to their agents.

## #2 · Inventory Mastery  [SCRIPTED — IN PRODUCTION]
**Runtime:** ~3 min · **Promise:** One catalog that runs your shelves, your web list, and the agent API — pricing, stock, and low-stock discipline.

**Script outline:**
1. The one-catalog idea: why three price lists (shelf, site, spreadsheet) always drift apart — and why agents punish drift instantly (they buy the stale price or walk).
2. Anatomy of a good item: short shelf label vs full name, unit clarity, blurb that a human skims and an agent parses.
3. Stock discipline: low-stock flags (<5), the out-of-stock shelf look, restock rhythm; stock is a promise two buyer types hold you to.
4. Price edits propagate: demo of an admin edit changing shelf, list, and JSON in one motion.
5. Habits: weekly count, kill dead SKUs, seasonal aisle planning.

## #3 · Selling to Humans AND Agents  [SCRIPTED — IN PRODUCTION]
**Runtime:** ~4 min · **Promise:** How agent buyers read your catalog, place orders, and pay in full with x402 USDC — while humans check out by card.

**Script outline:**
1. Meet the second customer: what an agent is, what it sees (JSON, not your pretty shelves), what makes it choose your store (clean data, stock truth, price clarity).
2. The x402 flow in plain English: order → 402 Payment Required → signed payment → facilitator verify/settle → USDC lands → order PAID. Full amount, no invoices, no chasing.
3. Humans in parallel: card checkout, same order list, `buyerType` tags; fulfillment is identical.
4. Reading your orders table: spotting agent buying patterns (bulk, repeat SKUs) and pricing for them.
5. Trust rules: never let the agent catalog and the human shelf disagree.

## #4 · Your Store Coin  [SCRIPTED — IN PRODUCTION]
**Runtime:** ~3 min · **Promise:** Design a loyalty-and-payment coin for your store — and the legal gates that come before any real issuance.

**Script outline:**
1. Why stores issue coins: the earn → return → spend loop and what it does to repeat purchase.
2. The maker wizard: name, ticker, supply, purpose (loyalty / payment / both) — design decisions and what each commits you to.
3. In-store mechanics: earn rates, what a coin buys, checkout acceptance alongside cards.
4. The gate, plainly: a coin is a regulated thing in most places. Platform rule — no issuance without per-business legal review. What the review covers (asset type, jurisdiction, consumer terms).
5. Launch checklist once cleared: mint spec from your saved design, customer terms, staff script.

## #5 · Marketing Your Virtual Store  [SCRIPTED — IN PRODUCTION]
**Runtime:** ~3 min · **Promise:** Shareable addresses, districts, and drops: getting shoppers — and their agents — through your virtual door.

**Script outline:**
1. Your address is your ad: the virtual address + URL everywhere your real address goes (receipts, packaging, socials).
2. District foot traffic: what neighborhoods and anchor stores do to discovery; events and shared drops with neighboring stores.
3. Marketing to agents: agents find stores through catalogs and directories — keep data clean, prices current, stock honest; your catalog IS your SEO for machines.
4. The scanned-store advantage: "walk through my real shop from your phone" as a story customers share.
5. Measuring: orders by buyer type tells you which marketing is working — humans browse, agents convert quietly.
